About

I approach problems before I approach code.

My work sits between research, business and software. I look for problems that cost people time or confidence, study the domain until I understand what a good answer looks like, and then design and build the tool that delivers it. AI-assisted development lets me move from idea to working product quickly; the thinking before it is what makes the product worth using.

Background

I studied psychology, which taught me to ask how people actually decide and behave, and to read research critically. degree, institution, years

I then worked in business and sales, where I learned to listen for the real problem behind a request and to explain value in plain terms. roles and industries to mention

Alongside that I kept coming back to data and technology: how information can be structured so it answers questions. Building products is where these meet. Carna, for example, draws on behaviour change, nutrition research and data engineering in equal measure.

My Process

The same four steps on every project, whether it is a personal tool or a client's workflow.

  1. Investigate

    Who has the problem, how often, and what it costs them. I look at the tools they already use and why those fall short, before deciding anything should be built.

  2. Research

    Studies, open datasets, standards and expert practice. I write down what the evidence supports, where it is thin, and which assumptions the product will rest on.

  3. Design

    Rules, data structures and workflows first, then the interface. I aim for the fewest screens and decisions that still do the job properly.

  4. Build

    A working version early, built with AI-assisted development and tested on real use. Then iteration: what people actually do with it decides what changes next.

What I Bring

Six things I can contribute to a project, each shown in the case studies.

  • Research and synthesis

    Reading across studies, datasets and expert sources, and condensing them into rules a product can follow.

  • Problem analysis

    Separating the problem someone describes from the one that is actually costing them, and sizing it honestly.

  • Product thinking

    Deciding what to build first, what to leave out, and how a feature earns its place for the user.

  • Data and systems

    Designing databases and pipelines that keep information clean, linked and ready to answer real questions.

  • UX and usability

    Interfaces that ask little of people: plain language, few steps, and clear feedback at each one.

  • AI-assisted development

    Building and testing working software quickly with AI tools, while keeping the logic understood and maintainable.
